01 / Why LibreEcho

Your speaker, on your terms.

Millions of capable Echo speakers are tied to a cloud service their owners never chose to depend on. LibreEcho gives that hardware a second life as an open, private speaker that works for you.

Music

AirPlay and Bluetooth

Play from your iPhone, Mac or any Bluetooth device. A built-in equaliser lets you shape the sound to your room.

Control

Your own control centre

Set up and manage your speaker from a web page on your home network. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to install on your phone.

Privacy

Local first

Wake-word detection runs on the speaker itself. You decide which services, if any, it talks to.

Peace of mind

Safe, signed updates

Updates are cryptographically signed and installed to a spare slot, so if anything goes wrong your speaker rolls back to the version that worked.

LibreEcho is an independent, volunteer-run project. It’s already in daily use, but it’s still young — some features are marked as experimental below, and we’re open about what isn’t finished yet.

03 / Supported devices

Which Echo do I have?

LibreEcho is built for specific hardware, so it’s worth checking your model before you begin. Devices that look similar, or share a name, can use quite different parts inside.

Check before you buy or download

  • Amazon Echo 2nd Gen — the tall fabric-covered cylinder released in 2017. Supported today.
  • Amazon Echo Dot 2nd Gen — the small puck. Supported from LibreEcho 0.14.
  • Other Echo, Dot, Show and Studio models aren’t supported yet — see the roadmap for what we’re looking at next.
  • The model is printed on the base of your device. If you share it with us, please leave out the serial number.

What’s new in LibreEcho 0.14

0.14 is the biggest update since our first stable release. It’s running on our test devices now and we’re working through the final checks.

In final testing

Headline changes

  • Echo Dot 2nd Gen support. One release now covers both the Echo 2nd Gen and the Echo Dot 2nd Gen.
  • A secure control centre. The web control centre is served over HTTPS, so your settings and passwords are encrypted on your network.
  • Sleep sounds. Built-in white, pink and brown noise with a sleep timer.
  • More wake words. A wider choice of wake-word models in the control centre.
  • Restart progress. When your speaker restarts, the control centre shows a progress bar and an estimate of when it will be back.

Under the hood

  • Smoother AirPlay with better LED ring feedback during playback.
  • A more robust way of installing and updating the system’s optional features.
  • Numerous fixes across Wi-Fi, audio, Bluetooth and the light ring.
  • An anonymous weekly check-in so we can see how many speakers run each release — exactly what’s sent is explained below.

When 0.14 is released, 0.13 will stop receiving updates. The full list of changes will be in the 0.14 release notes.

05 / Install

Installing LibreEcho, step by step.

Our installation guide for 0.13.19 covers every step in detail. Here’s an overview so you know what to expect before you begin.

01

Check your device

Make sure you have an Amazon Echo 2nd Gen, a computer, a USB data cable that you know works, and a small set of tools for opening the case.

02

Download the release

Download the files from the 0.13.19 release page and check they downloaded correctly using the checksum file. Keep all the files from the same release together.

03

Open and connect

Open the Echo and connect it to your computer over USB, following the 0.13.19 Echo 2nd Gen installation guide. The guide shows exactly where to connect.

04

Run the installer

A single installer checks your device and files, then writes LibreEcho. If anything doesn’t match, it stops before changing your speaker.

05

First start

Reassemble the Echo and power it on, then follow the guide to connect it to Wi-Fi and finish setup from your browser.

06

Stay up to date

Future updates install from the control centre — no need to open the device again. If an update ever fails to start, LibreEcho rolls back automatically.

Please read before installing: installation replaces your Echo’s software and erases what’s on it. It requires opening the device, and like any modification there is a small risk of damage. Follow the guide carefully, and ask on Discord if anything is unclear.

08 / Privacy

Local first, and clear about everything else.

LibreEcho is designed to do as much as possible on the speaker itself. When something does leave your home, it’s because you chose a service that needs it — or it’s one of the items below, which we explain in full.

Wake wordListening for the wake word happens on the speaker. Audio isn’t sent anywhere until the wake word is heard.
Speech recognition and repliesOn-device speech recognition and speech synthesis are available. If you choose an online service instead, it will receive your voice or text according to its own privacy policy.
Assistant servicesIf you connect an assistant, your requests go to that provider. Choose one you trust, and check its terms before turning it on.
MusicAirPlay and Bluetooth stream directly between your device and the speaker over your home network.
UpdatesYour speaker checks for updates when you ask it to, downloading them from the LibreEcho releases on GitHub.
Anonymous check-in (from 0.14)Once a week, the speaker sends a tiny message saying which model it is and which LibreEcho version it runs. That’s it — no name, account, Wi-Fi details, recordings or usage data. It helps us see how many speakers run each release, so we can roll out updates safely. Because it can’t be switched off, setup shows you exactly what’s sent before you finish.

About the mute button

We haven’t yet verified the mute button as a hardware-level guarantee that the microphones are disconnected. Until we have, please treat it as a software mute.

Optional diagnostics (from 0.14)

You can choose to share health information and crash reports to help us fix bugs. These are optional, are switched on by default for new installs, and can be turned off at any time in settings. Full crash logs are only sent if you choose to send one.

Keeping your speaker secure

  • Choose a strong admin password during setup.
  • Keep the control centre on your home network — don’t expose it to the internet.
  • When sharing logs, remove passwords, Wi-Fi names and serial numbers.

09 / Help and support

Need help? Here’s where to go.

LibreEcho is run by volunteers, so we can’t promise response times — but there’s a friendly community and we read every report.

Ask the communityThe quickest way to get help with installing or using LibreEcho. Join us on Discord.Report a bugFound something broken? Open an issue on GitHub with the steps to reproduce it.Suggest an ideaShare feature ideas and questions in GitHub Discussions.Report a security issuePlease report vulnerabilities privately through GitHub, not in a public issue.

Writing a helpful bug report

  • Your LibreEcho version, shown in the control centre.
  • Your Echo model (but not its serial number).
  • What you did, what you expected and what happened instead.
  • Whether it still happens after a restart.
  • Any logs, with passwords, Wi-Fi names and addresses removed.

Security updates

Our security policy covers every LibreEcho project. We aim to acknowledge reports within 7 days, assess them within 14 days and fix or mitigate confirmed issues within 90 days — on a best-effort basis.
