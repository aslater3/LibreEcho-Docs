01 / Why LibreEcho

Your speaker, on your terms.

Millions of capable Echo speakers are tied to a cloud service their owners never chose to depend on. LibreEcho gives that hardware a second life as an open, private speaker that works for you.

Music

AirPlay 2 and Bluetooth

Play from your iPhone, Mac or any Bluetooth device. Each speaker has its own tuned sound profile, with 24-bit output on the Echo 2nd Gen.

Control

Your own control centre

Set up and manage your speaker from a web page on your home network. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to install on your phone.

Privacy

Local first

Wake word, speech recognition and speech synthesis can all run on the speaker itself. You decide which services, if any, it talks to.

Peace of mind

Safe, signed updates

Updates are cryptographically signed and installed to a spare slot, so if anything goes wrong your speaker rolls back to the version that worked.

LibreEcho is an independent, volunteer-run project. It’s in daily use in homes today and gets better with every release. Where something is still experimental we say so below, and we’re open about what isn’t finished yet.

02 / Features

What works today.

An honest snapshot of each feature in the current release. Last updated 23 October 2026.

Music

  • AirPlay 2 from iPhone, iPad and Mac.
  • Bluetooth speaker with play/pause, track controls and phone volume sync.
  • Internet radio — add your own stations, including BBC radio streams.
  • Speaker tuning for each model, with 24-bit output on the Echo 2nd Gen.

Voice

  • On-device wake word, speech recognition and two built-in British voices.
  • Timers and “stop” work by voice with no internet connection.
  • Connect the assistant you choose: Home Assistant Assist, your own local AI server, or Wyoming speech services.
  • With an assistant connected, weather from the Met Office and other open sources.

Home Assistant

  • Found automatically through the ESPHome integration.
  • Use it as a voice satellite with Assist.
  • A media player for your automations, plus announcements and timers.
  • A microphone-mute switch you can automate.

Nursery

  • Listen in on the room from your phone’s browser, iPhone included.
  • White, pink and brown noise and a gentle heartbeat, with a sleep timer and fade-out.
  • A soft sleep light on the ring, solid or pulsing.

Your control centre

  • A web control centre for audio, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, timers, the light ring and updates.
  • Guided setup, multiple accounts, backup and restore, and one-click diagnostics.
  • A music visualiser, night mode and colour choices for the light ring.
  • A recovery Wi-Fi hotspot if your network ever changes.

Built to last

  • Signed updates from the control centre, with automatic rollback.
  • A documented REST API, with interactive docs on the speaker.
  • Optional SSH access for tinkerers.
  • Open source from the kernel up.

03 / Supported devices

Which Echo do I have?

LibreEcho is built for specific hardware, so it’s worth checking your model before you begin. Devices that look similar, or share a name, can use quite different parts inside.

Check before you buy or download

  • Amazon Echo 2nd Gen — the tall fabric-covered cylinder released in 2017. Supported today.
  • Amazon Echo Dot 2nd Gen — the small puck. Supported from LibreEcho 0.14, as an experimental target that has had less testing than the Echo.
  • Other Echo, Dot, Show and Studio models aren’t supported yet — see the roadmap for what we’re looking at next.
  • The model is printed on the base of your device. If you share it with us, please leave out the serial number.

Latest update

What’s new in LibreEcho 0.14.4

A fix update for every 0.14 speaker, released on 23 October 2026. Install it from the control centre: System › System update › Check for updates.

Available

Hears you better

  • Clearer voice pickup. The Echo 2nd Gen now steers its microphones towards whoever is talking, instead of a fixed mix that dulled speech from most directions. Fans and music don’t pull it away.
  • See where sound comes from. Audio › Microphones shows a live view of the direction sound is arriving from.
  • Runs cooler. On speakers that had taken many updates, the control centre could keep one processor core busy all the time. That’s fixed.

A softer light ring

  • Smooth fades. The sleep light, Bluetooth pairing pulse and other dim effects now breathe smoothly instead of stepping.
  • New start-up animation. A soft green glow rolls round the ring while your speaker starts.

Full details are in the 0.14.4 release notes.

Previous update

What’s new in LibreEcho 0.14.3

A sound-quality update for every 0.14 speaker, released on 20 October 2026. Install it from the control centre: System › System update › Check for updates.

Available

Better sound

  • Crackle-free Bluetooth. Bluetooth audio now goes through the same high-quality resampler as AirPlay, so the occasional crackle is gone.
  • 24-bit output. The Echo 2nd Gen keeps full precision from the volume control to the speaker, for cleaner quiet passages and vocals.
  • One volume for everything. Bluetooth and AirPlay share the same volume and the same path to the speaker.

Easier to use

  • Phone volume sync. Your phone’s volume buttons control the speaker, and changes made on the speaker show up on your phone.
  • Volume leveling (experimental). A new switch under Audio › Output evens out loudness between tracks. It’s off by default while we refine it for 0.15.

Full details are in the 0.14.3 release notes.

Out now

What’s new in LibreEcho 0.14

0.14 is the biggest update since our first stable release. It was released on 16 October 2026.

Available

Headline changes

  • Echo Dot 2nd Gen support (experimental). One release now covers both the Echo 2nd Gen and the Echo Dot 2nd Gen.
  • Home Assistant via ESPHome. Home Assistant discovers and adopts your speaker for voice, media, announcements and timers.
  • Internet radio. Stream stations such as BBC radio, and play music from a USB stick.
  • A more secure control centre. Turn on HTTPS in the control centre to encrypt your settings and passwords on your network.
  • The Nursery. Built-in white, pink and brown noise and nursery sounds, plus a baby monitor you can listen to from your phone.
  • Wake-word tuning. Adjustable sensitivity and microphone gain, and the option to use your own wake-word model.
  • Restart progress. When your speaker restarts, the control centre shows a progress bar and an estimate of when it will be back.

Under the hood

  • Smoother AirPlay with better LED ring feedback during playback.
  • A new, more robust update system: every update installs a complete, signed copy of the system, and your speaker keeps the previous one until the new one has started properly.
  • Numerous fixes across Wi-Fi, audio, Bluetooth and the light ring.
  • An anonymous weekly check-in so we can see how many speakers run each release — exactly what’s sent is explained below.

0.13 no longer receives updates. The full list of changes is in the 0.14 release notes.

05 / Install

Installing LibreEcho, step by step.

Start with the browser installer: it’s the simplest way in. If it doesn’t work for you, the installation guide for 0.14 covers the tested installer in detail, and there’s an overview of its steps below.

Please read before installing: installation replaces your Echo’s software and erases what’s on it. Like any modification there is a small risk of damage. Follow the guide carefully, and ask on Discord if anything is unclear.

Start here · preview, not fully tested

Try the browser installer first

The quickest way to install is straight from your web browser — there’s nothing to download or set up on your computer. Open the installer in Chrome, Edge or another Chromium-based browser on a desktop or laptop, plug in your speaker, and it takes you through each step.

It hasn’t been fully tested yet. Read the warning above before you start. If it doesn’t work for you, or you’d rather use the tested route, use the installer in the installation guide instead — the steps below describe it. Either way, please tell us how it went on Discord or in a GitHub issue.

Or use the guide installer

01

Check your device

Make sure you have an Amazon Echo 2nd Gen or Echo Dot 2nd Gen, a computer, and a USB data cable that you know works.

02

Download the release

The installer downloads the files from the 0.14.4 release page for you and checks every one of them before it changes anything on your speaker.

03

Connect

Connect the speaker to your computer over USB, following the 0.14 installation guide. No shorting or soldering of special points is needed — the guide shows exactly where to connect.

04

Run the installer

A single installer checks which model you have and that the files match it, then writes LibreEcho. If anything doesn’t match, it stops before changing your speaker.

05

First start

The installer restarts your speaker and opens the setup page in your browser, where you create your account and connect it to Wi-Fi.

06

Stay up to date

Future updates install from the control centre — no need to connect a cable again. If an update ever fails to start, LibreEcho rolls back automatically.

Already running 0.13? 0.13 can’t update itself to 0.14, because 0.14 uses a new update format. Your speaker stays safely on 0.13 until you reinstall with the 0.14 installer. Reinstalling erases your settings, Wi-Fi details and pairings, so you’ll run setup again afterwards. From 0.14 on, updates install from the control centre as before. Some Echo 2nd Gen speakers set up with early tools need a one-off boot-chain migration before reinstalling; the installation guide explains how to tell.

08 / Privacy

Local first, and clear about everything else.

LibreEcho is designed to do as much as possible on the speaker itself. When something does leave your home, it’s because you chose a service that needs it — or it’s one of the items below, which we explain in full.

Wake wordListening for the wake word happens on the speaker. Audio isn’t sent anywhere until the wake word is heard.
Speech recognition and repliesOn-device speech recognition and speech synthesis are available. If you choose an online service instead, it will receive your voice or text according to its own privacy policy.
Assistant servicesIf you connect an assistant, your requests go to that provider. Choose one you trust, and check its terms before turning it on.
MusicAirPlay and Bluetooth stream directly between your device and the speaker over your home network.
UpdatesYour speaker checks for updates when you ask it to, downloading them from the LibreEcho download mirror at dl.libreecho.org, or from the LibreEcho releases on GitHub.
Anonymous check-in (from 0.14)Once a week, the speaker sends a tiny message saying which model it is and which LibreEcho version it runs. That’s it — no name, account, Wi-Fi details, recordings or usage data. It helps us see how many speakers run each release, so we can roll out updates safely. Because it can’t be switched off, setup shows you exactly what’s sent before you finish.

About the mute button

We haven’t yet verified the mute button as a hardware-level guarantee that the microphones are disconnected. Until we have, please treat it as a software mute.

Optional diagnostics (from 0.14)

Setup asks whether you’re happy to share health information and crash reports to help us fix bugs. These are optional and can be changed at any time in settings. 0.14 doesn’t send either yet; they’ll only be used in a later release, and full crash logs will only ever be sent if you choose to send one.

Keeping your speaker secure

  • Choose a strong admin password during setup.
  • Keep the control centre on your home network — don’t expose it to the internet.
  • When sharing logs, remove passwords, Wi-Fi names and serial numbers.

Writing a helpful bug report

  • Your LibreEcho version, shown in the control centre.
  • Your Echo model (but not its serial number).
  • What you did, what you expected and what happened instead.
  • Whether it still happens after a restart.
  • Any logs, with passwords, Wi-Fi names and addresses removed.

Security updates

Our security policy covers every LibreEcho project. We aim to acknowledge reports within 7 days, assess them within 14 days and fix or mitigate confirmed issues within 90 days — on a best-effort basis.
